Silhouette: Austrian Titanium and the Rimless Standard

Silhouette is the brand most associated with rimless eyewear, and there’s a real engineering story behind that reputation. The company manufactures its own titanium in a factory in Linz, Austria, and assembles nearly every component in-house. This is unusual in an industry where most brands outsource production to keep costs down.

The rimless design is an aesthetic choice and more than that. Because there’s no frame holding the lens edges, the glasses weigh a fraction of what standard eyewear weighs, some models come in under two grams. Titanium is also naturally nickel-free, so it doesn’t trigger the skin reactions that some metal alloys cause on the nose and behind the ears.

Silhouette suits someone who wants their glasses to correct their vision without becoming the first thing people notice about their face. It’s a quiet, precise kind of luxury, built around comfort and engineering rather than statement-making design.

Face à Face: French Design With a Point of View

Face à Face sits at the opposite end of the spectrum from Silhouette, and that contrast is exactly why Austin Optics carries both.

Founded in Paris in 1995 by a small team with backgrounds in architecture and design, Face à Face treats each pair of glasses as a sculptural object. The brand builds its acetate collections from raw organic material and finishes them by hand in its own workshop in the Jura region of France, an area with a long history of eyewear craftsmanship. Selected pieces carry the Origine France Garantie certification, which verifies that the shaping, polishing, and assembly all happened on French soil.

What sets Face à Face apart visually is the layering. Two colors, two materials, deliberate asymmetry. A frame might combine a matte finish with a glossy one, or pair an unexpected color against a neutral base. This is a brand for someone who wants their glasses to read as a personal style statement.

What 100% Cotton Acetate Means

Acetate is the material most premium eyewear frames are made from, but not all acetate is the same quality, and this is an important detail many sites ignore.

Cheap plastic frames are typically injection-molded from petroleum-based plastic. They are less flexible, more prone to becoming brittle over time, and harder on sensitive skin. Cotton-based acetate, by contrast, starts as cotton fiber processed into sheets, which are then cut, shaped, and polished by hand rather than molded in a machine. The result is a material that’s more flexible, holds color and pattern better, and is generally more comfortable against the skin because it doesn’t carry the same petroleum byproducts.

Every frame Iris carries, across all five brands, is made from this cotton-based acetate. It’s part of why the frames feel and wear differently than what you’d find at a typical retail optical chain, and it’s a material distinction worth understanding before you compare price tags across different shops.
