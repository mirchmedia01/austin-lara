Why Customization is the New Standard

In the past, lens selection was limited to a few basic materials. Today, advanced digital surfacing technology allows us to “map” your prescription onto the lens with incredible accuracy. This results in:

  • Sharper Peripheral Vision: Reducing the “swim” effect often found in cheaper lenses.
  • Enhanced Contrast: Making colors more vivid and night driving safer.
  • Reduced Distortion: Especially important for high-power prescriptions or complex astigmatism.

The Impact of Proper Lens Selection on Visual Health

Lenses sit at the center of your visual experience. Even with the right prescription, poorly matched lenses can reduce contrast, distort vision, or cause fatigue.

Understanding how lenses interact with your eyes, prescription strength, and lifestyle helps you avoid unnecessary upgrades while investing in features that truly matter. The right choice improves comfort now and protects long-term visual health.

Factors That Influences Lens Selection

Selecting lenses starts with more than your prescription. It requires understanding how you use your eyes every day.

Vision Needs to Consider

Your optometrist evaluates:

Prescription strength and lens thickness needs

Pupillary distance (PD) and fitting height

Corneal vertex distance and base curve alignment

Presbyopia management and intermediate vision demands

High prescriptions may benefit from high-index lenses, while clarity-sensitive users benefit from designs that reduce chromatic aberration and improve contrast sensitivity.
