What Makes an Eye “Hard-to-Fit”?

For most, a standard soft lens works just fine. However, certain eye shapes and health conditions make traditional options uncomfortable or even ineffective. You might fall into this category if you have been told in the past that you aren’t a good candidate for contacts due to:

  • Irregular Corneas: Conditions like Keratoconus or scarring.
  • Astigmatism: When the eye is shaped more like a football than a basketball.
  • Dry Eye Syndrome: Where standard materials aggravate your symptoms.
  • Presbyopia: The need for multifocal solutions as you age.

Why One-Size-Fits-All Contact Lenses Fall Short

Standard contact lenses are designed for average eye shapes. But eyes aren’t one-size-fits-all.

When lenses don’t align properly with the eye, they can cause irritation, unstable vision, dryness, and long-term corneal stress. Hard-to-fit contact lenses solve these problems by addressing underlying eye conditions instead of masking them.

Knowing and understanding your options can mean the difference between giving up on contacts entirely and finding a solution that actually works.

What Makes Hard-to-Fit Contacts Different

Hard-to-fit contacts are custom-designed lenses created for eyes with unique shapes, surface irregularities, or specific medical needs. These lenses require advanced testing and expert fitting.

Common Reasons Standard Lenses Don’t Work

  • Irregular corneas or corneal ectasia
  • Keratoconus or post-LASIK regression
  • High astigmatism requiring precise toric lens alignment
  • Chronic dry eye or poor tear film stability
  • Surface irregularity affecting lens movement

Types of Specialty Contact Lenses

At Austin Optics, specialty contact lenses in Forest Hills may include:

  • Scleral lenses, which vault over the cornea and rest on the sclera, creating a liquid reservoir for comfort
  • Rigid Gas Permeable (RGP) lenses for sharper optics and durability
  • Hybrid lenses, combining soft lens comfort with RGP clarity
  • Multifocal custom lenses for presbyopia
  • Ortho-K (Orthokeratology) for overnight vision correction

The fitting process involves corneal topography or tomography, sagittal depth measurements, fluorogram pattern analysis, and over-refraction to fine-tune vision and comfort.
