Why a Contact Lens Exam Costs More Than a Regular Eye Exam

A general eye exam checks your overall eye health and determines your glasses prescription.
 A contact lens exam, however, requires additional measurements, tests, and personalized fittings that ensure your lenses are safe and comfortable.

Since contact lenses sit directly on the surface of your eye, your optometrist must analyze factors that go beyond a glasses prescription.

Typical Cost of a Contact Lens Exam

While prices vary by location and the type of lenses you need, most contact lens exams fall within the following ranges:

Basic Contact Lens Exam

Typically includes:

  • Eye health screening
  • Corneal measurement
  • Contact lens fitting
  • Trial lenses
     Estimated range: $50 to $150

Specialty Contact Lens Exams

Certain patients require specialty lenses, such as:

  • Toric lenses for astigmatism
  • Multifocal lenses
  • Rigid gas-permeable lenses
  • Scleral lenses

These examinations require more advanced testing and customization.
 Estimated range: $100 to $250+ depending on complexity.

First-Time Wearer Training

If you’re new to contact lenses, your exam may also include:

  • Lens insertion and removal training
  • Care and cleaning guidance
  • Wear schedule planning
     Estimated range: $20 to $50

What’s Included in a Contact Lens Exam?

Understanding what you’re paying for helps clarify the value of this specialized service.

1. Corneal Curvature Measurements

Your optometrist maps the shape of your cornea to determine the correct lens size and fit.

2. Tear Film Evaluation

Your tear quality and moisture levels determine how comfortably your lenses will sit on your eyes.

3. Trial Lens Fitting

You’ll be fitted with trial lenses to evaluate:

  • Positioning
  • Movement
  • Comfort
  • Clarity

4. Contact Lens Prescription

Unlike a glasses prescription, a contact lens prescription includes:

  • Base curve
  • Diameter
  • Lens type and brand
  • Material
  • Power

5. Personalized Lens Recommendations

Your optometrist may recommend:

  • Daily disposables
  • Bi-weekly lenses
  • Monthly wear lenses
  • Specialty lenses

6. Training for New Wearers

This ensures you feel confident and safe handling your lenses from day one.

Coverage varies, but many vision plans offer partial benefits for contact lens fittings.
 Insurance may cover:

  • A portion of the contact lens exam fee
  • The evaluation portion
  • Trial lenses
  • A set of contact lenses

How Often Do You Need a Contact Lens Exam?

Most patients need a yearly contact lens exam to:

  • Ensure the prescription is up-to-date
  • Monitor eye health
  • Adjust fit if your eye shape changes
  • Evaluate any signs of dryness or irritation

Contact lenses require ongoing monitoring to protect your corneas and maintain long-term comfort.

Signs You May Need an Updated Contact Lens Exam

If you experience any of the following, it’s time to schedule your exam:

  • Blurry vision with contacts
  • Frequent dryness
  • Redness or irritation
  • Reduced comfort
  • Difficulty removing lenses
  • Headaches or strain

These symptoms often indicate that the fit or prescription needs adjustment.
