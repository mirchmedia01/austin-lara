What Your Eye Exam at Austin Optics Includes

Visual Acuity Testing

The starting point. We measure how clearly you can see at different distances and determine whether your current prescription still meets your eyes’ needs.

Glaucoma Screening

Glaucoma is called the silent thief of sight. It can rob you of peripheral vision for years before you notice anything is wrong. Catching elevated eye pressure early is the only reliable way to get ahead of it.

Cataract Assessment

Cataracts develop gradually. Many patients do not realize how much their vision has changed until it is measured against where it used to be. We check the clarity of your lens and track any changes from visit to visit.

Retinal Health Evaluation

The retina lines the back of your eye and sends visual signals to the brain. Signs of macular degeneration, diabetic eye disease, and other conditions often show up here before a patient notices any change in their vision. This part of the exam matters most for patients who have been putting off a visit.

How Often You Should Get Your Eyes Examined

If you are under 40 and have no known risk factors, a comprehensive eye exam every two years is generally recommended. Annual exams are the better call if you wear glasses or contacts, have a family history of glaucoma, have diabetes or high blood pressure, or have not had a full exam in over a year.

Once you hit 40, the risk of glaucoma, cataracts, and macular degeneration starts to climb. Annual exams become the standard at that point, not the exception.

If you have not had a proper eye exam in the past year, that is the main thing to know. Come in.

Common Questions

Eye Exam Questions We Hear Often

How long does the exam take?

A full comprehensive exam typically runs 30 to 45 minutes, depending on what needs to be checked. It is not a quick visit, and it should not be.

Do I need a separate exam for contact lenses?

Yes. A contact lens prescription requires its own fitting exam, separate from your standard glasses prescription. Contacts sit directly on your eye, so the fit and curvature have to be assessed separately. We offer contact lens exams as a separate service.

Can I come in for an exam if I already have a regular optometrist?

Yes. We fill outside prescriptions from any licensed doctor. Some patients come to Austin Optics specifically because they want better lenses than their usual optician carries.

Does Medicare cover my eye exam?

 Medicare Part B covers medically necessary eye exams, including those related to glaucoma, cataracts, and diabetic eye conditions. Routine exams are generally not covered under standard Medicare, though supplemental and Medicare Advantage plans vary. Call us, and we will help you figure out what applies.

How do I know if I need a full eye health exam or just a prescription update?

 If it has been more than a year since your last full exam, or if you have a family history of glaucoma, diabetes, or high blood pressure, you need a full exam. A prescription update alone does not catch the conditions that develop without obvious symptoms.
